# Introduction

Artificial Intelligence enhances the Platform Partner Engine by helping organizations onboard faster, manage their public presence, improve operations, maintain compliance, understand performance, and use AsBeez capabilities more effectively.

AI should reduce administrative work and improve decision support without replacing authoritative business records, approved policies, compliance review, contractual consent, or human accountability.

Within the Platform Partner Engine, AI operates as an assistant, analyst, and advisor.

It does not become the legal representative of a Platform Partner, approve regulated businesses autonomously, accept agreements, alter paid subscriptions, or override platform governance.

---

# Purpose

The AI Capabilities component exists to:

- Simplify Platform Partner onboarding.
- Improve business profile quality.
- Assist with Storefront creation.
- Support verification and compliance review.
- Recommend suitable subscription plans and features.
- Analyze Partner performance.
- Identify operational risks and growth opportunities.
- Automate repetitive administrative work.
- Improve support and training.
- Enable governed AI agents for Partner operations.

---

# Guiding Principle

> **AI should help Platform Partners participate more successfully while preserving human authorization, business accountability, regulatory compliance, and the authority of platform policies.**

---

# AI Philosophy

The Platform Partner Engine uses AI to assist with understanding, preparation, analysis, and recommendation.

AI may:

- Summarize
- Extract
- Classify
- Translate
- Compare
- Predict
- Recommend
- Draft
- Detect anomalies
- Automate approved low-risk tasks

AI must not independently:

- Approve a Platform Partner.
- Verify regulated licenses as authoritative.
- Accept a Platform Participation Agreement.
- Change Platform Participation Fee terms.
- Activate a paid subscription.
- Publish regulated claims.
- Suspend or terminate a Partner.
- Modify immutable history.
- Expose confidential business information.
- Override an authorized human decision.

# AI Action Levels

AI capabilities should be classified according to their impact.

## Level 1 — Informational

AI explains or summarizes information.

Examples:

- Explain onboarding requirements.
- Summarize performance.
- Compare subscription plans.

No approval is normally required.

---

## Level 2 — Drafting

AI prepares content or a proposed change.

Examples:

- Draft a Storefront description.
- Prepare a compliance checklist.
- Suggest provider categories.

A human reviews before publication or submission.

---

## Level 3 — Low-Risk Automation

AI performs reversible, policy-approved actions.

Examples:

- Apply approved formatting.
- Tag a document.
- Schedule a reminder.
- Save a draft.

Actions remain auditable and may require configurable confirmation.

---

## Level 4 — High-Impact Action

Examples:

- Submit a Partner application.
- Publish a Storefront.
- Change a paid subscription.
- Add an authorized representative.
- Modify legal entity information.

Explicit authorization is required.

---

## Level 5 — Restricted Decision

Examples:

- Approve a Platform Partner.
- Reject verification.
- Suspend a Partner.
- Accept a PPA.
- Modify PPF terms.
- Terminate participation.

AI may provide analysis but must not independently execute these decisions.

---

# Human-in-the-Loop Controls

Human approval should be required for:

- Legal declarations.
- Agreement acceptance.
- Paid subscription changes.
- Regulated business approval.
- Compliance exceptions.
- Public regulated claims.
- Partner suspension or termination.
- Ownership changes.
- Banking or settlement changes.
- High-risk verification decisions.

Approval should preserve:

- Human approver
- AI recommendation
- Supporting evidence
- Decision
- Timestamp
- Reason

---

# Explainability

AI recommendations should explain:

- What was observed.
- Which information was used.
- Why the recommendation was produced.
- What uncertainty exists.
- What action is suggested.
- Whether human approval is required.

Example:

```text
Recommendation:
Reverification is recommended.

Reason:
The professional license associated with the Service Provider role expires in 21 days.

Source:
Compliance Record LIC-2026-143.

Suggested Action:
Upload the renewed license or temporarily restrict the affected Service category.
```

---

# AI Confidence

AI-generated outputs may include confidence indicators where useful.

Examples:

- High Confidence
- Moderate Confidence
- Low Confidence
- Human Review Required

Confidence must not be presented as certainty or legal validity.

Low-confidence extraction or classification should be routed for review.

# Authoritative Data

AI output is not authoritative merely because it was generated from platform data.

Authoritative records remain within their owning engines.

Examples:

| Information | Authoritative Source |
|---|---|
| Platform Partner Status | Platform Partner Engine |
| Product and Service Records | Commerce Engine |
| PPA and PPF Terms | Platform Participation Engine |
| Subscription Billing | Financial Engine |
| QPR and Allocations | Revenue Allocation Engine |
| Rewards | Rewards Engine |
| Licenses and Compliance Decisions | Approved Compliance Records |

AI may summarize or interpret authoritative information but must not replace it.

# Security

AI capabilities must enforce:

- Strong authentication
- Role-based access
- Relationship-based access
- Tool permission checks
- Prompt injection defenses
- Output filtering
- Secure model access
- Secret isolation
- Sensitive-data masking
- Rate limiting
- Abuse detection
- Audit logging

An AI assistant must never gain broader permissions than the user or service identity invoking it.

---

# Prompt Injection and Untrusted Content

Uploaded documents, Storefront content, websites, and external integrations must be treated as untrusted input.

AI systems should not follow instructions embedded inside:

- Uploaded files
- Product descriptions
- Web pages
- Compliance documents
- Images
- External API responses

These sources provide data, not authorization.

Only platform policies and authenticated user instructions may authorize actions.

---

# Fairness and Non-Discrimination

AI-supported verification, risk scoring, recommendations, and performance analysis must avoid inappropriate discrimination.

Models should not use protected or irrelevant personal characteristics to determine:

- Partner approval
- Risk classification
- Search visibility
- Subscription eligibility
- Performance recognition
- Compliance treatment

Models should be monitored for unequal outcomes and reviewed regularly.

---

AI services may consume approved Platform Partner Events.

Examples include:

- PartnerApplicationSubmitted
- PartnerVerificationFailed
- PartnerStorefrontPublished
- PartnerSubscriptionPastDue
- PartnerPerformanceDeclined
- PartnerComplianceDocumentExpired

AI may publish its own advisory events, such as:

- PartnerRiskIndicatorDetected
- StorefrontImprovementRecommended
- SubscriptionUpgradeRecommended
- ComplianceReviewRecommended
- PartnerChurnRiskDetected

AI advisory events must be clearly distinguished from authoritative business events.

---

# AI Events Versus Business Events

Example:

```text
AI.StorefrontImprovementRecommended.v1
```

means an AI system produced a recommendation.

It does not mean:

```text
Partner.PartnerStorefrontUpdated.v1
```

The Storefront changes only after an authorized business action succeeds.

---

# Auditability

AI interactions should be auditable when they:

- Read confidential Partner data.
- Produce high-impact recommendations.
- Invoke tools.
- Create drafts.
- Trigger notifications.
- Attempt state changes.
- Support compliance or verification decisions.

Audit records should identify:

- User or service identity
- AI Agent
- Data accessed
- Action requested
- Tool invoked
- Result
- Approval status
- Timestamp
- Correlation ID

# Business Rules

## PPE-AI-001

AI output is advisory unless an explicitly governed automation policy states otherwise.

---

## PPE-AI-002

AI must not approve, reject, suspend, terminate, or legally bind a Platform Partner.

---

## PPE-AI-003

AI must not accept a Platform Participation Agreement or modify PPF terms.

---

## PPE-AI-004

Paid subscription changes require explicit authorization.

---

## PPE-AI-005

AI may not expose information beyond the invoking user's permissions.

---

## PPE-AI-006

AI-generated public content must be reviewed before publication when required by Storefront or compliance policy.

---

## PPE-AI-007

AI-assisted verification results must preserve their source, confidence, and review status.

---

## PPE-AI-008

Low-confidence document extraction must not update authoritative records automatically.

---

## PPE-AI-009

High-impact AI recommendations must be explainable and auditable.

---

## PPE-AI-010

AI systems must use versioned models and governed prompts or instructions.

---

## PPE-AI-011

AI must not modify immutable Partner history, events, audit records, or financial records.

---

## PPE-AI-012

AI-generated events must be labeled as advisory unless they record an actual completed AI operation.

---

## PPE-AI-013

External content must be treated as untrusted and must not authorize platform actions.

---

## PPE-AI-014

AI agents must use the same APIs, validation, and authorization rules as human users.

---

## PPE-AI-015

Regulated, legal, financial, or compliance-sensitive decisions must remain subject to qualified human review.

# Long-Term Vision

The Platform Partner Engine should evolve into an AI-assisted business operating environment that helps every organization participate successfully in AsBeez.

A small local business should receive the same quality of digital guidance, analysis, compliance support, and growth intelligence that would traditionally require a large administrative and technology team.

At the same time, the platform must preserve trust by ensuring that AI remains transparent, governed, secure, and subordinate to authoritative business rules and accountable human decisions.

---

# Closing Statement

Artificial Intelligence enables the Platform Partner Engine to reduce friction, improve information quality, identify risks, strengthen Storefronts, explain performance, and guide business growth.

Its value comes not from replacing Platform Partners or administrators, but from helping them make better-informed decisions and complete platform work more effectively.

By combining intelligent assistance with strict governance, AsBeez can deliver scalable business support without compromising legal authority, privacy, compliance, or trust.

---

# Platform Partner AI Principle

> **AI should make Platform Partners more capable, not less accountable. It may prepare, explain, detect, recommend, and automate approved low-risk work, but legal authority, commercial consent, compliance decisions, and high-impact actions must remain governed by platform policy and authorized human judgment.**
