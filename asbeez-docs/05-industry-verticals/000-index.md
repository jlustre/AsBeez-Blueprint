# Industry Verticals

## Purpose

The **Industry Verticals** section defines the business domains supported by the AsBeez Marketplace ecosystem. Each vertical represents a specialized marketplace built upon the common AsBeez platform architecture while extending it with industry-specific business rules, workflows, data models, user experiences, integrations, and regulatory requirements.

Rather than building separate applications for each industry, AsBeez provides a unified commerce platform where every marketplace shares the same core infrastructure, including authentication, membership, commerce, financial management, rewards, AI capabilities, notifications, and analytics.

This approach allows businesses and customers to enjoy a consistent experience across multiple industries while enabling rapid expansion into new markets.

---

# Vision

To become the world's most comprehensive AI-powered multi-industry marketplace platform by enabling businesses and consumers to transact across virtually every legal industry through a single unified ecosystem.

---

# Objectives

The Industry Verticals architecture aims to:

- Support multiple industries using one common platform.
- Reuse shared platform services across all marketplaces.
- Provide industry-specific business capabilities.
- Accelerate development of new marketplaces.
- Maintain consistent user experience.
- Reduce development and maintenance costs.
- Enable cross-industry customer engagement.
- Increase revenue opportunities.
- Promote scalability and modularity.
- Future-proof the platform for emerging industries.

---

# Platform Philosophy

Every industry vertical should inherit the capabilities provided by the core AsBeez platform while implementing only the features unique to that industry.

Each vertical should remain:

- Modular
- Independent
- Configurable
- AI-Enabled
- API-First
- Event-Driven
- Globally Scalable

# Architectural Principles

Every industry vertical should:

- Reuse existing platform services whenever possible.
- Avoid duplicating business logic.
- Maintain loose coupling with other modules.
- Follow Domain-Driven Design (DDD).
- Follow Event-Driven Architecture (EDA).
- Expose well-defined APIs.
- Support configuration over customization.
- Be independently testable and deployable.
